Fluid Simulation — an interactive fluid playground in your browser

This is a free, real-time fluid simulation that runs entirely in your web browser. Drag your mouse or finger across the screen to paint glowing, swirling fluid and watch it curl, mix and fade like ink in water. There is nothing to download and no sign-up — it works instantly on desktop, tablet and mobile, and it's oddly satisfying to play with when you need to relax.

How to play

  • Click and drag (or touch and swipe) anywhere to inject fluid.
  • Space pause · S splash · C clear · H hide panel · F fullscreen.
  • Open the settings panel to change fluid type, colors, bloom and physics in real time.

Fluid presets

Each preset changes the physics and colors of the simulation. Jump straight into one:

  • Water simulation — light, swirly liquid with ocean colors.
  • Smoke simulation — wispy, turbulent plumes that drift and curl.
  • Lava simulation — slow, glowing fire that lingers like a lava lamp.
  • Honey simulation — thick, golden, viscous fluid.
  • Oil simulation — heavy rainbow swirls with long trails.

How the simulation works

Under the hood this page solves a simplified form of the Navier–Stokes equations for incompressible flow, computed entirely on your GPU with WebGL2 fragment shaders. Every frame the solver advects velocity and dye, runs a Jacobi pressure projection to keep the fluid incompressible, and applies vorticity confinement to preserve the small swirls that make real fluid dynamics so hypnotic. A bloom pass makes the bright dye glow.

FAQ

Is this fluid simulation free?

Yes — completely free, with no download, no account and no ads. It runs directly in your browser.

Does it work on phones and tablets?

Yes. The simulation supports touch and multi-touch, so you can paint fluid with several fingers at once on any modern phone or tablet.

How does the fluid simulation work?

Your GPU solves a simplified version of the Navier–Stokes equations in real time: advection moves the fluid, a pressure solve keeps it incompressible, and vorticity confinement keeps the swirls alive. It's the same technique used for smoke and liquid effects in games and films.

Can I change how the fluid behaves?

Yes. Pick a preset — water, honey, oil, lava or smoke — or open the settings panel and tune vorticity, pressure, fade, splat force, resolution, color palettes and bloom yourself.

Is this a good antistress or relaxation app?

Many people use fluid simulations exactly for that — the slow, colorful swirls are calming to watch and satisfying to control, like a digital lava lamp you can touch.

What technology powers it?

A single self-contained HTML file: vanilla JavaScript and WebGL2 shaders, no frameworks and no libraries. That's why it loads instantly.

Runs best in an up-to-date browser with WebGL2. Your drawings never leave your device.
